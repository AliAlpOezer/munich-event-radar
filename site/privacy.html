When you open the page

Hosting: Cloudflare

The page is served by Cloudflare Pages. Every request your browser makes carries your IP address, the date and time, the address requested, the page you came from, and your browser's identification. Cloudflare needs these to deliver the page and to protect it against attacks. Cloudflare, Inc. (USA) processes them on my behalf; I do not receive these logs. Cloudflare is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, which covers the transfer to the USA (Art. 45 GDPR). Cloudflare's privacy policy.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, my legitimate interest in delivering the page securely.

Event data: Supabase

Your browser loads the events and organisations from a database run by Supabase in Frankfurt, Germany. These requests carry your IP address and your browser's identification, and Supabase keeps them in its request logs for one day. Supabase Pte. Ltd. processes them on my behalf under its data processing agreement; where it uses providers outside the EU, the transfer is covered by the European Commission's standard contractual clauses. Supabase's privacy policy.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, my legitimate interest in showing the radar.

Event cover pictures

Some events show a cover picture that your browser loads directly from the organiser's or ticket platform's server, for example Luma, Eventbrite or a university's website. That server receives your IP address and your browser's identification; the page tells your browser not to send which page you were on. Each of these operators is responsible for what it does with the request.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, my legitimate interest in showing each event the way its organiser presents it.

Fonts, scripts and links

Fonts, scripts and the banner photo come from this site itself, not from Google Fonts or another third-party server. The one exception is Google's sign-in button, described below, which loads only once you open the login. Links to organisers and registration pages take you to those sites, where their own privacy policies apply.

If you log in

Logging in is optional. It lets you star events, keep your own applied, registered or skipped status on them, add events, and suggest organisations for the radar. There are two ways to log in, Google and GitHub; there are no passwords and no email login.

At Google or GitHub

The "Continue with Google" button is Google's own. When you open the login, your browser loads it from Google, which receives your IP address and your browser's identification and, if you are logged in to Google, can recognise you. Visitors who never open the login send nothing to Google. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR, logging you in as you asked; for the script itself, § 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG.

When you choose "Continue with Google" or "Continue with GitHub", you sign in on their site, and they ask whether to share your name, email address and profile picture with the radar. What they process there is their own responsibility: Google Ireland Limited and GitHub.

What is stored

Your account lives in Supabase's authentication service, in the same Frankfurt database:

WhatData
Your accountEmail address, name, the address of your profile picture, the provider (Google or GitHub) and your account ID there, your GitHub user name, when you signed up and last logged in
Your login sessionsThe IP address and browser identification of each session, and a log of logins and logouts with the IP address
What you do hereThe events you starred and when, your status on each event you marked, the events you added (the address of their page, and the day and place you gave) and the organisations you suggested (their web address and name), each with its progress

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR, providing the account features you asked for; for the session IP addresses and browser identification, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, my legitimate interest in keeping accounts secure.

Only I, as the operator, and Supabase, on my behalf, can see this data. Other users never see your email address, your stars or your statuses. Once I approve an organisation you suggested, automated jobs on GitHub Actions fetch and summarise its public pages, using an AI model through OpenRouter; they work with the organisation's pages, not with your account. An event you add is fetched and checked by such a job at once, and the AI model may read its page; once it passes, the event is on the public board without your name.

Stars are also counted per event: an event that 5 or more accounts starred is at least in the middle tier on the board, and one that 10 or more starred is in the top tier, with "starred by 5 or more" or "starred by 10 or more" as the reason. Nobody sees who starred an event or how many did. Legal basis for this count: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, my legitimate interest in showing first the events people keep.

How long it is kept

As long as your account exists. To delete it, email me from the address you logged in with: I then delete your account with your stars, statuses, sessions and login log. Events you added and organisations you suggested stay on the radar, no longer linked to you.
